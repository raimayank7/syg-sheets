Math and counting

COUNTIF and COUNTIFS functions in Google Sheets

COUNTIF counts the cells that meet one condition; COUNTIFS counts the rows that meet several.

COUNTIF answers "how many?" with a condition: how many passed, how many orders are pending, how many times a name appears. COUNTIFS adds more conditions.

Syntax

=COUNTIF(range, criterion)
=COUNTIFS(criteria_range1, criterion1, [criteria_range2, criterion2], ...)
ArgumentWhat it is
rangeThe cells to look at.
criterionThe condition: a value, a comparison in quotation marks, or a cell.

Examples

  • =COUNTIF(B2:B9,"8A")
    Result: 4

    Students in class 8A.

  • =COUNTIF(C2:C9,">=75")
    Result: 4

    Maths marks of 75 or more.

  • =COUNTIF(A2:A9,"*a")
    Result: 4

    Names ending in a.

  • =COUNTIF(C2:C9,">"&AVERAGE(C2:C9))
    Result: 5

    Marks above the average.

  • =COUNTIFS(B2:B9,"8A",C2:C9,">=60")
    Result: 3

    Two conditions: class 8A with at least 60 in Maths.

  • =COUNTIFS(C2:C9,">=40",C2:C9,"<60")
    Result: 2

    A band: from 40 up to 59.

  • =ARRAYFORMULA(COUNTIF(B2:B9,{"8A";"8B"}))
    Result:
    4
    4

    In ARRAYFORMULA, COUNTIF counts for each item of a list.

How they work

COUNTIF checks each cell of a range against a condition and counts those that pass. COUNTIFS takes pairs of range and condition and counts the rows where all of them hold.

Conditions are written as for SUMIF: text and comparisons in quotation marks, cell references joined on with &, and the wildcards * and ?.

Useful special cases:

  • "" counts empty cells, and "<>" counts cells that are not empty.
  • "?*" counts cells containing at least one character of text.

Duplicates

  • =COUNTIF(A:A, A2)>1 is TRUE for any value that occurs more than once. As a conditional formatting rule (Format, Conditional formatting, Custom formula is), it colours the duplicates.
  • =COUNTIF(A$2:A2, A2) numbers each occurrence, 1 for the first, 2 for the second.

Sheets also offers Data, Data clean-up, Remove duplicates for deleting them.

Is a value in another list?

=COUNTIF(Other!A:A, A2)>0 tells whether A2 appears in the other tab. It is the simplest way to compare two lists.

A count for every item, from one formula

Inside ARRAYFORMULA, COUNTIF accepts a list of criteria, as the last example shows. Combined with UNIQUE it makes a frequency table in two cells:

  • H2: =UNIQUE(B2:B)
  • I2: =ARRAYFORMULA(IF(H2:H="", "", COUNTIF(B2:B, H2:H)))

One formula does it all with QUERY: =QUERY(A1:E, "select B, count(A) where B is not null group by B", 1).

COUNTIFS, like SUMIFS, does not expand over a list of criteria.

Counting different values

To count how many different values a range holds, use COUNTUNIQUE, described on the UNIQUE page. COUNTUNIQUEIFS adds conditions.

Counting by a pattern or a calculation

COUNTIF needs a real range and a simple condition. For a test on a calculation, such as the month of a date or a text pattern, count the rows that FILTER returns, or add up a test:

  • =COUNTA(FILTER(A2:A13, MONTH(A2:A13)=3))
  • =SUMPRODUCT(REGEXMATCH(B2:B13, "(?i)^a"))

Tick boxes

A column of tick boxes holds TRUE and FALSE. =COUNTIF(D2:D50, TRUE) counts the ticked ones, which makes a quick progress counter for a task list.

Common mistakes

  • Comparison signs outside the quotation marks.
  • COUNTIFS ranges of different sizes.
  • A band that loses its edge, such as ">40" with "<60" when 40 should count.
  • Stray spaces, so that "8A " is not counted as "8A".
  • Expecting an empty cell to pass ">=0". It does not.

In Excel

COUNTIF and COUNTIFS are the same in Excel. COUNTUNIQUE is not available there.
